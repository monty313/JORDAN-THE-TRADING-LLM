## The three rules, as spoken

1. The good trade is a hard trade.
2. Fade the short-term trend in the direction of the long-term trend.
3. Place your entries where the masses put their stops.

## What the channel claims each rule means

**Rule 1.** The discomfort is the point. The clip attributes the line to Michael Steinhardt. Price often looks like it is still falling, or still rising, at the moment a pullback entry is right. The channel says that feeling comes from a liquidity sweep and from inducement: a break that looks real, then fails, because the pullback never reached a deep enough discount. They treat a retracement that does not reach 38.2% of the impulse as invalid, and they prefer 50% or deeper. That percentage is their cutoff. Jarvis does not adopt it.

**Rule 2.** Higher-timeframe trend first. Buy the pullback, do not chase the impulse. David Paul calls that shape an AB=CD. The channel then teaches higher highs and higher lows, a break of structure as continuation, and a break of the major low as the reversal. Premium is the upper half of the range. Discount is the lower half. Buys belong in discount. Sells belong in premium. Direction without location is not an entry.

**Rule 3.** The crowd's stop is the fuel. Enter after those stops have been taken, not at the obvious place where you would have parked your own stop. Their mechanical version draws a Fibonacci on the last impulse and parks a limit at 70.5%, halfway between 61.8% and 78.6%, with the stop a few pips beyond the swing, and the first target in front of the next higher-timeframe level. At 1:1 they take half off and move the stop to flat. They also stack that limit with a fair value gap, with a market-structure shift, or with a lower-timeframe trigger inside a higher-timeframe zone. They say a 50% account loss needs a 100% gain to get back to even, so survival outranks a hot setup.

## What already agrees with this desk

The thesis chain is the same shape as rule 2: higher-timeframe force, then the pullback, then the lower-timeframe release. A chase of the impulse is the easy trade rule 1 warns against. Rule 3 agrees with a structural stop and with refusing an entry that has not yet shown the crowd was wrong. Waiting is a real act.

## What this video does not get to change

The 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 70.5%, and 78.6% levels are the channel's recipe. S5 chop and expansion stay UNDEFINED. Fair value gaps, breaker blocks, and order blocks are not S1–S4. They do not flip `act`, `tide`, or a direction conflict. The Euro-dollar five-minute example in the video is an illustration, not a live signal. New orders stay `SAFE_HOLD`.
