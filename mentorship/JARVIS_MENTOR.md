## 3. Pullback and continuation are different trades

This is the sentence Mark added, and it is the part of the mentorship that the baseline leaves as a heading. Two timeframes are the difference. A single chart cannot tell them apart, because on one chart a dip and a breakout can look like the same excitement.

### Pullback

The higher timeframe is already on a side. The slower sensor is still on that side. The faster sensor, or the price on the tight band, has moved the other way and has not yet come back. That state is loaded. It is not an entry.

The entry is the release: the fast sensor crosses back to the slow side while the slow sensor and both higher timeframes are still there. David Paul says the same shape in ordinary language. Fade the short-term trend in the direction of the long-term trend. The good trade feels hard, because at the moment of the dip the short-term chart still looks like it is going the other way. The easy trade is buying the thing that is already running.

On this desk the pullback vehicles are S1 and S2, sets 1 and 2.

- S1, Dual CCI slingshot. Both higher timeframes have CCI 30 and CCI 100 above their shifted averages (buy; sell is the mirror). On the anchor, CCI 100 stays above its shifted average while CCI 30 falls below it. That is the load. Fire when CCI 30 rejoins, still with the higher timeframes intact. Wait while CCI 100 is fine and CCI 30 is still on the wrong side of its average. Kill when CCI 100 loses the side, either higher timeframe loses the side, or the anchor starts crossing its average back and forth.
- S2, Dual Bollinger pullback. Both higher timeframes hold price outside the wide and the tight band on the trend side. The anchor stays outside the wide band and closes through the tight band the other way. That is the breath. Fire when price reclaims the tight band and the wide containment is still true. Kill when the anchor accepts through the wide band, or the 50-period average no longer supports the side.

Applied prices, because a wrong price silently changes the act. S1 CCI uses typical price, `(high + low + close) / 3`. S2 bands, the S2 price comparison, and the 50-period average use the close. A stamp that says every row is typical price is wrong for S2.

The stop sits where that topology becomes false. For a pullback, that is beyond the extreme of the breath, far enough that the spread is not most of the stop. A Fibonacci percentage is not that place. The crowd's stop, in David Paul's third rule, is the fuel you wait to see taken. It is not a limit price Jarvis computes at 70.5 percent.

### Continuation

Both timeframes are already on the same side, and the fast sensor is with the slow sensor, not against it. Price is leaving with them. There is no breath to buy. There is a move that is already underway.

That is S3, the shifted envelope. Both higher timeframes are outside the forward-shifted high and low rails. The anchor is outside its own rails. A wick through the tunnel is not acceptance. The candle body has to clear it. If that clearance comes with expanding width after a prior load, it is a release. If it comes with expanding width and no prior load, it is a launch.

A launch is not a pullback that was entered late. Fading it because CCI 30 or RSI 2 looks stretched is the mistake the baseline names and the account has already paid for in other costumes: treating excitement as an entry. S3 is a legal official strategy. It was not the mentorship test. The mentorship test was the hard trade, the pullback. Using a launch to "complete" a pullback lesson would erase the lesson.

### The one-line test

Read the higher timeframe first. Then read CCI 100. Then read CCI 30.

| Agrees on a side | Still on that side | Against that side | Pullback, loaded | Wait. Name the rejoin that would release it. |

A fractal print does not fill any cell of that table. A MACD cross does not fill any cell of that table. A 0.5-deviation band break does not fill any cell of that table.

Fire when RSI 2 turns back out of that extreme. This is a fast snap inside a trend that is already allowed.
